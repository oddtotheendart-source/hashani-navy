Modern Ship Buying For The Discerning Captain
By Kuriel Al'Jafri

THE SHORT VERSION

Usually the most cost-effective way to buy a new ship is to collect the
maximum number of shiplines and shipcloth allowed for your desired ship
type in HELP SHIPTYPES. As long as those commodities are outside of your
rift when you COMMISSION, you will receive a discount on the total cost
and you will be quoted a price about 70 percent of the total commission
cost.

The aforementioned applies in the era in which I am writing, in which
rope and cloth are plentiful, iron and steel are only moderately
expensive, and wood prices are ridiculously high.

AN EXAMPLE

Let us say you wish to purchase a brand new strider. Without any
commodities, it will cost you 5 million gold. With all of the suggested
commodities on HELP SHIPTYPES, it will cost you 1,250,000 gold. Because
of many considerations explained below, the bottom line is that you'll
want to collect 300 shiplines and 300 shipiron (the maximum allowed in
HELP SHIPTYPES), remove those 600 commodities from your rift, WALKTO
MAELSTROM and COMMISSION SEASTRIDER. Instead of the full price of 5
million gold, he will quote you 3,950,000 gold. Unless you have access
to free wood, iron, and steel, this is currently your best discount.

THE BRIEF DETAILS

Unless comm prices drastically change, you'll want to exclusively
collect shiplines and shipcloth and pay for the rest of the ship out of
pocket. Here's what you'll need:

Cutter: 1.5K Iron, 7.5K Cloth, 7.5K Rope

Stride: 3K Iron, 15K Cloth, 15K Rope

Galley: 6K Iron, 30K Cloth, 30K Rope

Bring those comms to any shipfitter, and ask them to make the maximum
amount of shipcloth and shiplines available to the ship of your choice
in HELP SHIPTYPES. Outrift those, walk to your ship vendor, commission
the vessel, and see the discount.

THE FULL DETAILS

The full cost of each ship type, without any shipcomms, is:

Cutter: 2.5 million gold
Strider: 5 million gold
Galley: 10 million gold

But when commissioning a ship, each shipcomm that you are holding (not
in your rift) is assigned a gold value that is deducted from the total
cost of the ship, as long as the number of shipcomms that you're using
does not exceed the figures in HELP SHIPTYPES. In the ship market, each
shipcomm is valued at:

Shipwood: 3000 gold
Shipiron: 2250 gold
Shipcloth: 1750 gold
Shiplines: 1750 gold
Pitch: 3000 gold

That is not remotely its market value, because a shipcomm is a composite
of normal commodities prepared by a shipfitter. Here's the conversion
rate:

Shipwood: 50 wood and 10 iron
Shipiron: 50 iron and 10 steel
Shipcloth: 50 cloth
Shiplines: 50 rope and 10 iron
Pitch: 50 wood and 10 animal fat

As you may already have noticed, the ship market seriously undervalues
most commodities. Although 1 shipwood deducts 3000 gold from your ship
purchase, in the current market it would cost something like 10,000 gold
to obtain 1 shipwood. However, it also overvalues some commodities. 1
shipcloth deducts 1750 gold from your ship purchase, but at current
market prices it would only cost 900 to purchase 1 shipcloth. Therefore,
it pays to know the comms market, and to have a general idea of where
shipcomms save money and where they cost it.

Below is the total breakdown of comm costs for each ship type, converted
to conventional comms so that you can easily check prices via CM LIST.
Since we do conclude that, in most cases, you're best off buying just
shipcloth/shiplines and paying for the rest in gold, the conversion rate
for just that method is also included below.

CUTTER
Max Shipcomms: 100 pitch, 200 shipwood, 200 shipiron, 150 shiplines, 150
shipcloth
Total Comms Cost: 15K Wood, 13.5K Iron, 2K Steel, 7.5K Cloth, 7.5K Rope,
1K Fat
Total Shipcloth/Shiplines Alone: 1.5K Iron, 7.5K Cloth, 7.5K Rope

STRIDER
Max Shipcomms: 200 pitch, 400 shipwood, 400 shipiron, 300 shiplines, and
300 shipcloth.
Total Comms Cost: 30K Wood, 27K Iron, 4K Steel, 15K Cloth, 15K Rope, 2K
Fat
Total Shipcloth/Shiplines Alone: 3K Iron, 15K Cloth, 15K Rope

GALLEY
Max Shipcomms: 400 pitch, 800 shipwood, 800 shipiron, 600 shiplines, 600
shipcloth
Total Comms Cost: 60K Wood, 54K Iron, 8K Steel, 30K Cloth, 30K Rope, 4K
Fat
Total Shipcloth/Shiplines Alone: 6K Iron, 30K Cloth, 30K Rope

Unfortunately, the formula for figuring out the value that the ship
market places on any one commodity requires a few simultaneous
equations, none of which are pretty. For the mathematically inclined,
they are:

Shipwood: 300 gp= 5*wood + iron
Shipiron: 225 gp= 5*iron + steel
Shipcloth: 175gp= 5*cloth
Shiplines: 175gp= 5*rope + iron
Pitch: 300gp= 5*wood + fat

It would make little sense to solve any of these equations in a scroll,
because the market value of each commodity is constantly in flux. But
given the current market value of any commodity, it should be
easy enough to use the above equations to figure out if you happen to
have found a good deal, assuming you're buying all of the comms needed
for that particular shipcomm. The mathematically inclined will note that
there's no real way of figuring out the objective value of any of those
comms except for cloth (35gp). The even more mathematically inclined may
figure out a way and make me look pretty silly for saying that.

Special prize to anyone who can figure out, based on all of this, the
ship market value of each commodity. Until then, I leave you with the
unsolved math and wish you fair winds and following seas.

Swashbucklers wander around your ship to protect your shipmates (and

assigned a station either and CAN get trapped behind closed doors.

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Personnel
Though not necessarily the only option you have, the following are the
crew numbers recommended in general for a successful ship. The numbers
are presented in a format of Crewmates/Shipmates/Swashbucklers.

For a Windcutter: 24/15/3  (72000g to hire)
For a Seastrider: 60/40/5 (225000g to hire)
For a War Galley: 70/50/6 (275000g to hire)

2. New Crew
While training and moralising your crew, it is recommended that you pay
them generous wages and activate Helm aura at the maximum level to keep
their spirits up and help them get the most out of every journey. The
fastest way to boost morale is by cooking stores with Helm aura
maximised. The fastest way to gain experience is by killing seamonsters,
which is also good for morale.

3. Crew Training & Upkeep
Crewmates and shipmates will gradually gain experience from performing
their rowing and repairing duties. Swashbucklers only gain experience
from seamonster kills, crew experience trades, and plundering or being
plundered. Morale needs to be maintained by keeping the crew fed and
paid.
Empty stores or strongboxes will harm morale.

4. Base Wages - The End Goal
Once a ship's crew is fully moralised, their wages can be reduced
gradually, but you will need to compensate for decreased morale each
time you lower them. The minimum pay levels or "base wages" are 4 bits
for crewmates, 6 bits for shipmates, and 2 gold, 4 bits for
swashbucklers. If you top up morale to maximum after dropping the wages
to these levels, it will not decrease anymore due to low wages.

If you are going to be shooting things/people at sea, you are going to
need ammo!

Currently, we are allowing citizens to buy ammo from the Navy. If this
status should change, this scroll will be altered to reflect it.

You are required to leave a minimum of 1000 ammo of any time on the Navy
ships. If it is below this, please alert the Admiral and do not take any
of that type from that ship.

We have presently set the price for ammunition at 100 gold per unit.
This is significantly cheaper than broadly available market price.

But wait! What if you want to actually donate extra ammo you have
gathering dust to the Navy so that Hashani can buy it and our Navy can
be strong and full of things to shoot at people? Please just go ahead
and store it on any Navy ship and message the Admiral so they can say
thank you.


Useful information on ship ammo:

DEPOSIT ## GOLD HN <reason>

SHIP AMMO STATUS

SHIP AMMO REMOVE ## <ammo type>

SHIP AMMO STORE <ammo type> (no number!)

Please do not hesitate to ask the Admiral or any Maritime Aides any
questions you have regarding this. Happy sailing! Set them all on fire.

clhelp newship
Composed by: Kuriel on or about the 16th of Glacian, 829 AF.

Congratulations on your new ship! This checklist will help ensure that
you get the new vessel fully outfitted and ready for the rigors of the
sea, at minimal cost and with only slight inconvenience. As ever, make
sure to read the various clan scrolls and to ask for help when
necessary. We also strongly recommend new captains pursue at least Petty
Officer status in the Navy, via CLHELP PROGRESSION, to fill out any gaps
in their sailing knowledge.

CREW

1) See CLHELP CREW, for relevant numbers.
2) Visit Thraasi, Tasur'ke, or Shastaan and HARBOUR CREW FORHIRE to see
how many are available to hire. Use HARBOUR commands to hire. We
recommend hiring the maximum crew on all vessels.
3) You can hire crew to any ship, from those harbours. So even if your
ship is docked in Tasu'rke, you can hire crew from Shastaan. Presumably
they walk to your ship on their own.
4) Check SHIP CREW from anywhere on the ship, to confirm status.

STORES

1) The basic harbour commands are HARBOUR PURCHASE # TOKEN STORES FOR
shipname and HARBOUR PURCHASE # COMMON/GOOD/FINE STORES FOR shipname.
2) We recommend purchasing token stores equal to your total number of
crew, times three. We recommend purchasing 10 common stores initially,
to get your crew eating.
3) If you have access to bait fish, cooking is often a better option for
filling your stores, because it increases crew morale (especially in
conjunction with the helm aura) while also filling your larders. But the
specifics of cooking and increasing morale are beyond the scope of this
scroll.
4) Use the guidelines in HELP CREW to set rations. Better rations
increase morale faster.
5) Check SHIP STORES from the quarterdeck or cabin, to confirm status.

STRONGBOX

1) We recommend placing no more than 20-30k gold in your strongbox, to
prevent very large plunders from enemy ships cleaning you out entirely.
2) In the cabin, SHIP STRONGBOX DEPOSIT # gold.
3) Use the guidelines in HELP CREW to set wages. Better wages increase
morale faster.
4) SHIP STRONGBOX CHECK in the cabin, to confirm status

AMMO AND WEAPONS

1) Ask the Navy clan for low prices on ammunition. We sell at a fraction
of any market value.
2) Review HELP SHIP WEAPONS for the costs. Consider nondecaying your
weapons with SHIP REQUISITION NONDECAY weaponname.
3) SHIP AMMO anywhere on the ship will allow you to store, remove, or
check what you have. SHIP WEAPON STATUS anywhere on the ship will
confirm location and status of all weapons.

FIGUREHEAD

1) Review CLHELP SEASPELLS for instructions

NAMING AND FLAGGING

1) Disembark, with a bottle of strong drink on hand
2) SHIP NAME to name your ship
3) Board, and use SHIP SET ALIAS to create a short name for your ship
4) Obtain a flag with a recognisable coat of arms. Hashan sells Hashani
flags, with our city arms over purple or violet, which we recommend all
allied ships fly at all times
5) SHIP HOIST FLAG, from the quarterdeck or bow.

clhelp seaspells
Composed by: Minkai on or about the 19th of Mayan, 832 AF.

Seaspells

Seaspells are useful abilities that are gained 423 lessons into
the Seafaring skill, excepting the Hunger seaspell which you gain
automatically. They can be used to speed your journey from port
to port, escape or hide from pirates, or chase after fleeing
ships yourself.

In order to use seaspells, you must first obtain a figurehead.
These are sold in a variety of places in a variety of shapes. At
first, your figurehead will simply be a block of carved wood. In
order to empower it with the ability to use seaspells, you must
take it to the Altar of Sea Spirits and consecrate it.

The Altar of Sea Spirits is located roughly southwest of Zaphar's
port. After disembarking, take the figurehead to the altar, place
it on the gorund, and PRAY TO SEA SPIRITS. After a while, the
figurehead will be consecrated, and hold a modest amount of
power. Take it to the bow of your ship and SHIP MOUNT
<figurehead>. From that point on, you can meld with the ship by
standing at the bow and using INVOKE SEA SPIRIT. You can issue
orders while melded as if you were on the quarterdeck, if you are
the captain. RELSEASE SEA SPIRIT or move away from the bow in
order to break the connection.

Seaspells require a certain amount of power in the figurehead in
order to use. Power is restored to a figurehead by sailing - the
more you travel over water, the more power will build up. If a
figurehead is completely drained of power, it will be destroyed.
Resetting figureheads will merely pop off the bow and require
reconsecration. It's important to track your figurehead's power,
through TOUCH FIGUREHEAD while melded, so that you don't
accidentally destroy it.

Most seaspells must be invested into a figurehead in order to use
them. Investing can only be done while in a port or harbour,
though you can check what seaspells have been invested at any
time. There are eight investable seaspells, of which you are only
able to use four. There are two pairs of seaspells that are
mutually exclusive with each other. Investing one spell means
being unable to invest the other. These are Shipcloak and
Wavecall, and Shield and Rainstorm. Hunger is a special seaspell
that does not require investing to use.

== HUNGER ==
    INVOKE HUNGER <seamonster>
    Power Cost: 300

    This seaspell is specially designed to be used while hunting
    seamonsters. It casts a spectral net upon the targetted
    seamonster and, should the seamonster die before the net
    fades, the figurehead that cast the net will be replenished
    by a significant amount of power. This helps to replenish
    figurehead power during long seamonster hunts.
NOTE: This seaspell can only be used on level 2.5 or higher
    seamonsters. You must be melded with the figurehead to use this
    spell.


== Shipcloak ==
    INVOKE CLOAK
    DISSOLVE CLOAK
    Power Cost: 250

    Surrounds your ship in a cloak of darkness, preventing it
    from being seen by other ships. Any flares shot nearby will
    dissolve the cloak and reveal you. As well, moving while
    cloaked will still allow nearby sailors with shipwarning to
    see that a ship is moving nearby, but won't reveal the name
    of your ship.


 == Wavecall ==
    INVOKE WAVECALL <direction> <distance between 1-8>
    Power Cost: Varies

    This useful seaspell will catapult your ship instantly in
    whatever direction you specify, by however many metrons you
    specify, up to eight. It doesn't change the direction or the
    speed at which your ship is moving, even when wavecalling in
    the opposite direction. Use of this ability costs both power
    and personal endurance, and damages the hull, all of which
    will increase with the distance chosen. Also, if any nearby
    sailors have shipwarning up, they will be able to see your
    ship use wavecall, as well as the direction you went in. Be
    aware that you cannot wavecall while grappled, and in the
    presence of some high level seamonsters.
    NOTE: You must be melded with the figurehead to use this spell.


== Shield ==
    INVOKE SHIELD
    Power Cost: 200

    Raises a shield around your ship to protect it from damage
    attacks. Another ship using a wavescythe spell can shatter
    it.


== Rainstorm ==
    INVOKE RAINSTORM
    Power Cost: 50

    Summon a rainstorm to douse all decks and sails of fires at
    once.


  == Barrier ==
    INVOKE BARRIER
    DISSOLVE BARRIER

    Power Cost: 300

    Provides prismatic barriers around all aboard your ship. Once
    invoked, it will continually create barriers that are lost,
    but at the cost of more power from the figurehead. You must
    dissolve the barrier to stop them from being made.


  == Windboost ==
    INVOKE WINDBOOST
    Power Cost: 150

    A very handy seaspell, it temporarily boosts your ship's
    speed.


  == Whirlpool ==
    INVOKE WHIRLPOOL <direction>
    Power Cost: 200

    Using this seaspell summons whirlpools temporarily around your
    ship. Passage into and out of these whirlpools is slow and
    difficult, making it easier to run from someone chasing you
    that gets caught in one.


  == Wavescythe ==
    INVOKE WAVESCYTHE <target ship>
    Power Cost: 200

    Shatters the magical shield of another ship within range.

Shipfitters (TRADEWHO SHIPFITTING) can dye sails and paint, stain, or
trim ship hulls. These modifications are visible when you SHIP LOOK from
aboard the ship, SHIP LOOK <dir> at an adjacent ship, or SHIP LOOK
<alias> while standing at the harbour. Dyed sails are visible on
shipwarning.

Ships that have already been prettified using an artefact pail must be
restored before they can be further modified. A shipfitter can restore
the ship using ship commodities.

To decorate a ship, you will need shipfitting supplies that are made
from regular comms. A shipfitter can prepare the following:
Shipwood:  50 wood and 10 iron
Shipiron:  50 iron and 10 steel
Shipcloth: 50 cloth
Shiplines: 50 rope and 10 iron
Pitch:     50 wood and 10 animal fat

To dye the sails of a ship, you will require a mixture of red, blue and
yellow pigments and these base costs:
Windcutter: 15 shipcloth. (750 regular cloth)
Seastrider: 30 shipcloth. (1500 regular cloth)
War Galley: 60 shipcloth. (3000 regular cloth)

To paint/stain a ship, you will require a mixture of red, blue and
yellow pigments and these base costs:
Windcutter: 5 shipwood. (250 wood 50 iron)
Seastrider: 15 shipwood. (750 wood 150 iron)

 STATUE OF NERAEOS FYLAKAS
---------------------------
Chiselled from pale marble, the figure of Neraeos Fylakas towers above
the waters below. Thin veins of blue and grey streak across the surface
of the statue, crossed by flickering darts of light reflected by the
surrounding waters. Taking the form of a warrior born, He stands atop a
massive, fluted column with His shoulders thrust back, and His weight
resting upon His right leg, the pillar of Duty and Vigilance. Depicted
in full battle regalia, He wears a finely textured tunic and kilt
beneath a muscled breastplate, with greaves covering His arms and legs.
A sash of blue-green silk drapes across the midsection of the statue,
knotted in front with the ends hanging loosely, signifying His rank
among the triton warriors. His right shoulder dropped slightly, He holds
ready the Shield of Tides, and in His left hand, He wields a mithril
spear. A magnificent spine-crested helm crowns His head, the marble
gleaming as brightly as the bronze it imitates.

 STATUE OF NERAEOS PELAGIA
---------------------------
Seated upon a rendition of the Thrones of Oceans, the marble figure of
Neraeos Pelagia looks upon His realm with piercing eyes, His countenance
holding an ageless, contemplative expression. Beneath the crown atop His
head, curly hair tumbles to His shoulders, and His braided beard is rife
with bits of seaweed and small, colourful shells. Bare-chested aside
from the small pendant that graces His form, the Thalassan God-King
seems almost human in appearance. With one hand resting upon the purple
skirt trimmed with golden thread that covers His lap, the other holds
aloft a replica of the Trident of the Seas. The symbol of the ruler of
the Tides glows dimly, light pouring forth from flowing script of the
Tritons that wraps around the bronze weapon.

 STATUE OF THE CASPEID NOTIA
-----------------------------
Standing atop the skeleton remains of a great Leviathan, a gleeful
likeness of Notia, carved from marble and veined in streaks of beautiful
violet amethyst, keeps her gaze upon the sprawling skies of Sapience. A
prominent dorsal fin runs along the length of her back, parting her
Long, flowing hair. Clad only in a silk dress, one arm is left bare upon
which a slender, golden armlet in the shape of a coiled sea serpent
rests. Webbed fingers clasp a net woven of silver thread in one hand and
an ornate pitcher in the other, both raised overhead as if in offering
to the heavens.

 STATUE OF THE CASPEID EUSIA
-----------------------------
Standing twenty feet high, this colossal statue is made of the purest
white marble, honouring the matron of the eastern kingdoms. Clothed in a
white dress of woven seagrass and almost touching the ceiling, the
visage of Eusia looks down upon her waters with a gentle but equally
powerful expression of knowing vigilance. Although her eyes lack pupils,
her countenance weighs heavily with an overwhelming look of sadness. Her
long fingers and bare toes, webbed with translucent skin remain
outstretched and end in curved, sharp talons.

 STATUE OF THE CASPEID SEFYR
-----------------------------
Set upon a mound of crushed seashells and glimmering pearls, a grey,
seaworn statue of the Western Guardian gazes across the Sapphire Sea.
Carved into the likeness of a ruggedly handsome humanoid clothed in a
short kilt of finely woven seagrass, the figure poses in a seated
position, his knees bent up slightly with his arms resting across them,
both webbed hands clasping daggers reminiscent of a manta ray's stinger.
Gills lining his neck, along with curiously slitted vertical pupils give
him a very fish-like appearance. The welcoming smile of Sefyr is belied
by the three rows of razor-sharp shark teeth that fill his mouth.

 STATUE OF THE CASPEID BORAK
-----------------------------
Embedded within the top of a massive glacier and sculpted from deep blue
quartz, a statue of the Northern Caspeid bears the likeness of a
muscular, young man clothed in a short kilt of finely woven seagrass.
Long hair drapes his solemn face, ending where his matted, spiky beard
begins. A row of sharp, dorsal spines lines his back from the base of
his neck to his hips. Finely webbed fingers grasp a slender and elegant
silver trident in one hand and an ornate pitcher filled with hailstones
in the other. Full of anger, icy blue eyes show him to be as grim and
serious as the waters he gazes upon.
