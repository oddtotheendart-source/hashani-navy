5. Deep Sea Diving - techniques, tips, and tricks
1.  Leadlines are your friend. Any shipfitter can make one for you.
      TRADEWHO SHIPFITTING. It requires 10 leather and 40 rope.
      SHIP TAKE SOUNDING will tell you ahead of time if it's a good
      location to dive and deep sea fish.
2.  If you do not have a diamond-tipped shovel, take extras. They will
      break.
3.  Post one person topside deep sea fishing to serve as look out.
4.  Do not raise the bell until everyone is accounted for.
5.  You can return topside as soon as you are in the bell, while it
      remains on the ocean floor just SHIP LEAVE BELL. You cannot
      re-enter the bell until it's topside.
6.  You do not have to raise the bell the entire way each time you dive.
      Once you see brightness you can SHIP LOWER BELL. "The bell
      continues its ascent into the brightness above."
7.  Recommendations: Kill the aggressives, they are more likely to drop
      a key. Kill the other creatures. Dig the entire area. Then if you
      still have not found a key and have a steel-encased strongchest,
      TURN CHEST HANDLE RIGHT.
8.  Most of your dives will be one bell. Anything above one bell is more
      likely to have a sea dragon. Those happen once a month, per sea
      floor area.
9.  When you cook, severed fingers are worth good gold, if one falls
      out of the sea creature keep it, AB SHIPFITTING SCRIMSHAW.
10. Pale bone chests - good gold, lots of risk. You open these up with
      the skeleton keys made through shipfitting. At the moment you
      unlock the chest, you have a random chance of summoning a sea
      monster. Beware, I did not summon a level 1, I summoned a level 5.
      2 chomps and my cutter was sunk. So get the gold and put it in
      your pack, FAST and take necessary steps for this potential event.
11. CLHELP DEEPSEATREASURE in the Hashan Navy clan gives you a list of
      the items you might dig up, when digging on the ocean floor.
12. All deep sea creatures can be used to cook; except dragons, spiders,
      hippocampi, and merrow.
13. You can butcher all creatures before cooking them.
14. Activating Helm Aura level 5 is 20 pts to crew morale per creature
      as you cook them.
15. Deckhand level 1 or 2 adds 200 common food to larder per creature.
      Deckhand level 3 to 5 adds 200 good food to larder per creature.

clhelp kraken
Composed by: Icraa on or about the 21st of Mayan, 923 AF.

Icraa's quick tips on fighting the kraken
- you want to start the fight, firing at TENTACLE and not SEAMONSTER,
since you might find yourself trying to aim for the kraken itself while
one of its tentacles is wrapping itself around your ship

- go around near the edge in a circle, most of the times everyone does
this in a clockwise motion

- try to stay at the max of ballista range, the kraken really hurts up
close (and rip you off the figurehead) but cant even touch you if you
are far enough away

- if you find yourself getting hit by the kraken, your ONLY priority is
to get outside his hitting range, if your hull gets low dont be afraid
to go to the very edge and anchor down for repairs, and keep an eye on
the incoming kraken, as it most likely have not lost sight of you

- having one on your ship hitting with starshots and spidershots can
make it a bit easier should you be so unfortuante to find yourself
inside the reach of the kraken

- finally, every ship that survives the battle also gets a tentacle, so
pace yourself according to your own skill level, try to stay afloat and
good luck!

reach out.

clhelp seamonsterareas
Composed by: Rhydian on or about the 8th of Glacian, 883 AF.

Seamonsters can occasionally surface anywhere at sea. However, they are
concentrated in certain areas, as listed below.

EASTERN

Caspite Reaches in the Borean Ocean - large area east of Falaq'tor,
north of Valho. To avoid, once you are parallel with Valho harbour
travel northeast until you can go no farther. Follow the easternmost
channel north. These are level 4 and higher monsters.

Bay of Dardanos - Huge area west of Minos. Draw an imaginary line from
Tenwat harbour south, and stay east of it. These are mainly level 3
monsters.

Scyrian Reaches, directly east of Tasur'ke past the hard chops then NE
to the statue of Lord Neraeos. These are mainly level 3 monsters.

Southern Riparian Reaches, Northern Phocian Reaches. Medium sized area,
directly east of Ulangi. It is between and amongst the slow go chops.
This area is a level 5 area, windcutters beware.

Eastern Phocian Reaches. Medium sized area, south-southeast of
Zanzibaar. Drop south from the Zanzibaar harbour and as soon as you
clear land head east. It's about 40 metrons east. These are level 4
monsters.

Notic Ocean. Medium sized area, directly east of Zaphar Isle. These are
level 1 monsters.

WESTERN

Notic Ocean. Smallish area, south-southwest of Tapoa. These are level 2
monsters except for the occasional Kashari raider, which is level 3.

Sapphire Sea. Medium sized area, directly northwest of the Ashtan
Harbour. Level 1s.

Northern Sapphire Sea. Medium sized area, directly west of Phereklos
harbour. Level 3s.


NORTHERN

Borean Ocean. Smallish area, directly north of Karbaz.

Borean Ocean. Tiny area, along the coast directly north of the Vents of
Hthrak.

clhelp seamonsters
Composed by: Micharoj on or about the 17th of Ero, 914 AF.

Seamonsters can be killed by shooting discs from a thrower and darts
from a ballista at the monster. You can not target a seamonster like
another ship, you must FIRE <weapon> AT SEAMONSTER. Darts and discs both
count as the same thing, one shot.

Firing from an onager:

Spidershot   - Decrease the damage of a seamonster's next attack.

Starshot     - Slow a seamonster's attack speed for a short time.

Chainshot    - Slow a seamonster's movement speed for a short time.

Flares - Stop a monster moving for a short time, fired from any weapon

Chainshot and flares are useful if you are trying to escape a randomly
drawn seamonster while sailing somewhere.



Seamonster hunters like to classify monsters on different levels. Each
level has a specific amount of shots from darts/discs to kill it.

Level 1 - 20 shots
Level 2 - 25 shots
Level 3 - 30 shots
Level 4 - 40 shots
Level 5 - 60 shots

For quick reference:


1 - Flying Sheilei, Foam-Wreathed Sea Serpent, Mudback Septacean,
Red-Faced Septacean

Beware of close-ranged attacks on hull and sails (rarely both), very
rarely sail entanglement, and little on-board adventurer damage.

Monsters here take 20 hits to die, and trophies turn in for 5000 gold.

--NESTS:
There are no currently known nests for this type of monster, however
they can be found through random encounters.


2 - Mass of Sargassum, Gargantuan Angler Fish, Gargantuan Megalodon,
Raging Shraymor

Beware of mid-ranged attacks on hulls and sails (often both), common
sail entanglement, and low on-board adventurer damage

Monsters here die within 25 hits, and trophies turn in for 7500 gold.

--NESTS:
Southwest of Tapoa, around the statue (occasionally spawns level 3
raiders)


3 - Furious Sea Dragon, Pirate Ship, Red-Sailed Kashari Raider, Trio of
Raging Sea Serpents

Beware of mid-ranged, strong attacks on hulls and sails (often both),
common sail entanglement, disruption of shipmelding, and low-medium
on-board adventurer damage.

Monsters here take 30 hits to die, and trophies turn in for 15000 gold.

--NESTS:
Southwest of Tapoa, around the statue (only raiders)
*Northeast of Clockwork Isle, around the statue in the chops
*South of Tenwat Harbour
*BEWARE that these nests have a low chance of spawning a surprise
leviathan


4 - Monstrous Ketea, Monstrous Picaroon, Sea Hag, Unmarked Warship

Beware of long-ranged, strong attacks on hulls and sails (often both),
common sail entanglement, disruption of shipmelding and dragon-level
on-board adventurer damage.

Monsters here take 40 hits to die, and trophies turn in for 50000 gold.

--NESTS:
*East of Zanzibaar, south of the statue
*West of Phereklos
*BEWARE that these nests have a low chance of spawning a surprise
leviathan


5 - Hulking Oceanic Cyclops, Legendary Leviathan, Towering Oceanic Hydra

Beware of long-ranged, very strong attacks on hulls and sails (often
both), common sail entanglement, disruption of shipmelding, preventionof
wavecall, and dragon-level on-board adventurer damage.

Monsters here take 60 hits to die, and trophies turn in for 100000 gold.

--NESTS:
South of Clockwork Isle, through the straits


Observed seamonsters include:

FLYING SHEILEI (level 1 - 20 shots)
The serpentine body of this sheilei shimmers with translucent golden
scales, measuring more than nine feet in length. Its tail fin nearly
doubles the length, however, lashing back and forth like a cat's tail. A
pair of broad, flat pectoral fins extend outward like aqueous wings,
delicate in appearance yet powerful enough to propel the creature out of
the water. The most striking feature of the monster, however, is its
broad jaw and enormous mouth, filled with three rows of razor-sharp
teeth that curve and twist, locking together like a tangle of briars.

FOAM-WREATHED SEA SERPENT (level 1 - 20 shots)
Almost a league in true length, the serpentine motions of this great sea
snake hides half of its sinuous coils beneath the surface of the waves.
Iridescent fins frill along the undulating body, enabling propulsion
through the deep waters of the ocean and churning up pallid seafoam with
their passage above and beneath the surf. Akin to its landbound cousins,
but much multiplied in size, the monster's head is that of the classic
snake's in shape, but larger than a troll. Black eyes stare dully out
from the blue mottled scaling, and a great forked tongue tastes the
tidal spray, flickering from between a terrifying set of fangs.

MUDBACK SEPTACEAN (level 1 - 20 shots)
Covered with a mottled pattern of dark brown shades, the back of this
creature appears to be covered in mud. Seven fleshy tentacles protrude
from its underbelly, flashing yellow and green as they writhe around its
form. A pair of beady black eyes is set near the base of the glob-like
body, and a sharp, beak-like mouth periodically emits a shrill,
punctuated shriek.

RED-FACED SEPTACEAN (level 1 - 20 shots)
Little more than a flat black glob of slime, the distinguishing feature
of this tentacled creature is a patch of crimson surrounding two beady
eyes. Seven serpentine tentacles twitch beneath the shapeless body,
curling and twisting as it searches constantly for prey.

MASS OF SARGASSUM (level 2 - 25 shots)
Coloured an ugly brownish-green and composed of countless blades of
algae, this mass of sea grass moves about at the whim of the ocean's
current, carrying broken wood and debris along with it. Clutched in the
briny grasp of the sargassum are numerous bloated bodies, each one
devoid of eyes and in various states of decay. In some places, openings
exist where fish circle about to feed on the rotting remains.

GARGANTUAN ANGLER FISH (level 2 - 25 shots)
The ghostly lantern hanging before it from an arcing, bony spine casts
an eerie glow over the sea, illuminating the terrible visage of this
enormous fish in spectral green light. Jagged teeth, each easily the
length of a seastrider's bowsprit, protrude from its fell, yawning maw

SHIP TRADES

1. What are Ship Trades?
2. Ship Trades Restrictions
3. Routing for Trades
4. Tips on Trading
5. How To Trade
6. Trading on City Ships


1. What is Ship Trading?

Ship trading is accepting a trade from either the harbour at Thraasi or
the harbour at Tasur'ke, and completing it as instructed. Trades change
each month at Serenade, when new ones will be offered. Once you have
accepted a trade, there is no time limit to completing it. The only
restriction is to make sure you have accepted the trade before Serenade
of the month it was offered. To see what can be gained from ship
trading, see CLHELP TRADEREWARDS.

2. Ship Trade Restrictions

Ship trades are restricted in two ways. Personal-restricted trades are
those that an individual captain can only complete once. If a trade is
personal, then you could gather up enough cargo to do the trade multiple
times, but it would have to be completed by different people.
Ship-restricted trades are those where that specific ship can only
complete it once. One person can do a ship-restricted trade multiple
times if they do it on a different ship each time. Some trades carry
both restrictions, so neither the person nor the ship can repeat it
within the month.

3. Routing for Trades

Okay, you've found a trade that offers something you want, so now what?
You know what you need to end up with, but how do you work out what you
need to start with, and what do you need in between them? Good resources
are CLHELP TRADEDEALCHART, CLHELP TRADEMATH, CLHELP TRADECARGO, and
CLHELP TRADEROUTEGUIDE.

4. Tips on Trading

You do not have to accept a trade before working on it. You can also
accept a trade in the middle of the route when it is most convenient to
stop at the harbour offering the trade. Or, if the trade is accepted and
completed at the same harbour, you can arrive with the correct cargo
already on-hand to accept and immediately complete the deal.

If you have a large enough ship, you can buy double or triple the cargo
and take a friend or two along for trades that are personal, but not
ship, so you can enjoy the trip together. You will need to sail back and
forth from the starting to ending harbour for each person to accept and
complete.

Two pieces of ship equipment, the cargo buoy and cargo float, can be
helpful on a ship you are planning to use for frequent trading. CLHELP
SHIPEQUIPMENT has details on these.

5. How To Trade

HARBOUR INFO at Tasur'ke or Thraasi will show the available trade there.
SHIP TRADE while on a ship will list out all required commands.

6. Trading on City Ships

City ships are available for trading on a first-come, first-served
basis. Please make sure to check a ship's log to ensure it has not
already done a ship-restricted trade. If you accept a trade on a city
ship, complete it as quickly as possible since the ship will not be
available for anyone else to use for trades until yours is complete.

Remember to replenish the strongbox with all cargo costs and docking
fees incurred throughout your trip.

Calculating your Trade Routes in (a lot) of (fairly easy) steps!
by Rhydian Malicieux


I. Introduction
Ship trading is one of those things that seems simple at first, but can
be a bit confusing to the uninitiated. Although the official and clan
help scrolls are a great place to start, it sometimes helps to see that
information in a different format. I found that charting it out like
this before I started helped a great deal.
So let's get started!

II. Preparation
It's always good to prepare for your route beforehand. That way, you
have a ready-made list of where you need to go and what cargo you need
to pick up.
Get yourself a pencil and a nice big sheet of parchment. You will want
to spread this sheet out atop your desk - it will help you excel in
creating your tradelists.

Look at the clan trade help scrolls (the tradedealchart is very helpful
here) and jot down the following information:
(don't worry, I'll show you how to arrange it in just a bit!)
Starting Harbours
Mid-route Harbours
Ending Harbour
The cargo you will be trading, and the ratios (4:3, 3:2, etc.)
Put this information into down onto your spread-out sheet of parchment
like so. For this example, we're going to use the following trade deal:
"Deliver 5 marble to Tasur'ke Harbour in return for crew morale (ship)."
Harbour | Inbound Cargo |  # In   | # Out   | Outbound Cargo | Ratio |
Shastaan| (nothing)     | XXXXXXX |         |  Sandstone     |  n/a  |
Tasur'ke| Sandstone     |         |         |  Granite       |  4:3  |
Karbaz  | Granite       |         |         |  Marble        |  3:2  |
Tasur'ke| Marble        |         | XXXXXXX |  (nothing)     |  n/a  |
This should be fairly self-explanatory, but I'll go over it right quick:
Harbour - A stop along your trade route.
Inbound Cargo - The TYPE of cargo you are bringing INTO a harbour.
# In - AMOUNT of cargo you are bringing INTO a harbour.
# Out - AMOUNT of cargo you are LEAVING with.
Outbound Cargo - TYPE of cargo you are LEAVING with.
Ratio - The ratio of exchange between two cargo types.
III. Calculations
For our example above, the calculations are fairly straightforward.
1. Put a number into the first "# Out" box. We'll use "12" for this
example.
2. Next, put that same number in the "# In" box on the line just below
it.
3. Take that "# In" number and find the "# Out" number as so:
  # Out = (# In) / (Ratio as a fraction)
For this first one, it would be:
  # Out = 12 / (4/3)
        =  9
There you go - if you bring 12 Sandstone from Shastaan to Tasur'ke, you
will be able to exchange it for 9 Granite. The calculation for the trade
at Karbaz is similar, just change the numbers and ratio:
  # Out =  9 / (3/2)
        =  6
At this point, just take that final "# Out" and stick it in the row
for your final stop - and you're done!
Harbour | Inbound Cargo |  # In   | # Out   | Outbound Cargo | Ratio |
Shastaan| (nothing)     | XXXXXXX |   12    |  Sandstone     |  n/a  |
Tasur'ke| Sandstone     |   12    |    9    |  Granite       |  4:3  |
Karbaz  | Granite       |    9    |    6    |  Marble        |  3:2  |
Tasur'ke| Marble        |    6    | XXXXXXX |  (nothing)     |  n/a  |
So, by using this method, we can see that on this route, if we leave
Shastaan with 12 Sandstone, we can end up back in Tasur'ke with 6 Marble
- enough for our trade! Of course, we will have a few extras left over
at the end, but that's no worry - more for next time!

IV. Conclusion
This technique can be applied to any trade route - just write it out as
shown here, and play with the numbers by starting with your first
outbound cargo and working your way down. Remember to start out with the
correct amount of cargo at the beginning, to get as few leftovers as
possible.

Thanks for reading, and may the trades be ever in your favour!

VOYAGES

This is a starting guide to voyaging on the high seas!

Voyages or "seafaring quests" can be requested by a captain in harbours
from dockworkers or harbourmasters:
SAY REQUESTING A VOYAGE FOR SHIP <#>

The ship in question has to be docked at the harbour where the quest is
being requested, the person requesting it must have assumed command of
said ship, and any crew looking to join need to be on the ship with CREW
permissions set.

Voyages will award gold and renown to everyone involved and seafaring
talisman pieces usually only to the captain (with some exceptions).

QUEST LOG OCEANS can be checked to see any voyages a person has signed
up for, and it's important to note that while there are ways to get more
voyages at the same time, a captain may not request a voyage if they
already have one.

Some voyages can only be requested at certain times (after new year, or
on a full moon), and some can only be obtained at a certain harbour, and
some only become available once you've completed the prerequisite
voyages.

See HELP VOYAGES for more details!
